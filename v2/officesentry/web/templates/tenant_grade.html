The rules

the same for every client
  1. Five parts. The checks are grouped into Admin accounts, Sign-in and accounts, Mail, Devices, and Sharing and apps. Checks about cost, capacity and housekeeping don't count.
  2. A part is its worst open finding. A critical finding makes it an E, a high finding a D, a medium a C, only low findings a B, and a part whose every check passed is an A. Accepted risks are set aside first, and info findings are notes, not problems.
  3. A part needs half its checks. It's only graded when at least half its checks could run (no Intune, no Entra ID P1, nothing collected yet); otherwise it says why, and the grade comes from the parts that were.
  4. The grade is the worst part. One critical gap keeps a client out of A and B. Fix the checks at a part's worst level and it takes its next grade.
  5. History is the grade on each month-end for the last twelve months, from the findings open then (first seen by then, not fixed, not an accepted risk), with each finding's current level and the parts graded today.
  6. The comparison sets a client beside the others of about the same size by licensed users, or every other client when fewer than {{ min_peers }} are that size: the median grade and figures, and how many it is better than. No client is named, and nothing leaves this install.
